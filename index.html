Intro

This site is a collection of notes, experiments, and observations about gpose in Final Fantasy XIV — composition, lighting, post-processing, and the visual language behind screenshots.

You absolutely do not need ReShade, paid shaders, 8k textures, high polygon models, or complicated setups to make great gposes. Some of the best screenshots are simple shots taken at the right moment with the right feeling behind them.

The best gpose is the one you were excited to take.

The tools and techniques described here are not meant to prescribe a “correct” look, but to help you better understand how images work and give you more control over bringing your own artistic vision to life.

Effects

Post-processing effects are not “make my screenshots look good” buttons. They are tools that can help shape mood, guide focus, simulate camera and lens behaviour, or compensate for some of FFXIV’s rendering limitations.

Each page explains:

  • what an effect is trying to simulate,
  • what artistic purpose it can serve,
  • and how different settings change the final image.

Most examples and settings breakdowns use the iMMERSE shader stack from Marty’s Mods because its controls map closely to concepts from real cameras and lenses familiar to me from photography.

Wherever possible, I also list alternatives — including older, free, or built-in effects — because good screenshots come from intentional choices, not expensive shader stacks.

Composition

Composition is the invisible structure underneath a screenshot. It decides what the viewer notices first, how the eye moves through the frame, and whether the image feels calm, dramatic, intimate, lonely, cinematic, or chaotic.

This section will focus on framing, subject separation, focal length, posing, visual balance, and the small choices that turn a screenshot from “a character standing somewhere” into an intentional image.

Lighting

Lighting is mood. The same pose and location can feel soft, ominous, romantic, nostalgic, or theatrical depending entirely on how the scene is lit.

This section will cover light direction, contrast, colour temperature, vanilla lighting tricks, relighting, and how to use light not just to illuminate a subject, but to shape emotion and atmosphere.

Editing

A screenshot does not necessarily stop being shaped the moment you press Print Screen. Cropping, grading, grain, tonal adjustments, and aspect ratios can completely change how an image feels.

This section will focus on subtle editing workflows, film-inspired looks, black-and-white grading, and the final polish that turns a screenshot into a finished image.
